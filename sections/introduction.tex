\section{Introduction}

Consider a set $\cH$ of $n$ \emph{hypotheses}, a set $\cT$ of $m$ \emph{tests}, and an unknown \emph{target hypothesis} $\target\in\cH$ that needs to be discovered through testing.
Each test $t\in\cT$ is a partition of $\cH$, that is, $t$ consists of subsets of $\cH$ such that for any different $X,Y\in t$,  $X\cap Y=\emptyset$ and $\bigcup_{X\in t} X=\cH$.
As a result of executing a test $t\in\cT$, the \questioner receives a \emph{reply} that reveals $H\in t$ such that $\target\in H$. Upon receiving this response, the \questioner adaptively selects the next test from $\cT$ to perform until the target hypothesis is identified (i.e., the intersection of all reply sets is a singleton). The goal is to conceive a strategy of testing, often modeled as a decision tree, which either minimizes the worst-case or the average-case number of tests performed until $\target$ is identified. We refer to the former problem as the \textsc{Worst Case Decision Tree} and to the latter as the \textsc{Average Case Decision Tree}. If the cost criterion is not mentioned explicitly, we refer to the problem as the \textsc{Optimal Decision Tree} ($\ProblemDT$).

In this work we generalize the above setup by considering instances which are subject to arbitrary precedence constraints between tests given as a partial order $(\cT,\preceq)$.
Hence, a valid strategy may perform a test $t\in\cT$ only after all its predecessors have been performed. We call such a strategy \emph{precedence-closed}. Precedence constraints also arise in scheduling, where their effect on complexity has been extensively studied.
On the applicability side, $\ProblemDT$ includes numerous use cases e.g. in medicine and biology \cite{BayesianLearnerOptimalNoisyBinarySearch}.
For example, hypotheses may represent possible conditions or diseases, and tests may represent medical procedures. Such a setup suggests including precedence constraints in various forms. For instance, some tests may be more invasive or expensive than others, and should be performed only after cheaper or less invasive tests have been performed. Additionally, it can also happen that a given test is a prerequisite for another test, e.g., a blood test may be required before performing a biopsy. Finally, a large test might consist of multiple stages, each with its own outcomes, with later stages performed only after earlier ones.

Algorithmically, the aforementioned precedence constraints make the problem significantly more challenging. In a classical setup (without such constraints) the usual way of proceeding is to select at each step a test that maximizes some measure of information gain (e.g., reduction in entropy or number of hypotheses pairs). However, when precedence constraints are present, it may happen that the most informative test cannot be chosen at the current step, as some of its predecessors have not been performed yet.
To address this challenge, we use a series of algorithmic `reductions' between optimization problems.
Of particular interest to us becomes set covering with precedence constraints:
We are given a set $\cU$ of $n$ items, a collection $\cS$ of $m$ subsets of $\cU$, such that $\bigcup_{S\in\cS}S=\cU$, a partial order $(\cS,\preceq)$ on these subsets and a parameter $0<f\leq 1$.
We say that a subfamily $\cC\subseteq\cS$ \emph{covers} a $f$-fraction of items from $\cU$ if $\spr{\bigcup_{C\in\cC}C}\geq f\cdot n$.
We ask for a $\cC\subseteq\cS$ that covers a $f$-fraction of items in $\cU$ and is \emph{precedence-closed}, that is, for each $x\in\cC$ and each $y\in\cS$ such that $y\preceq x$ it holds $y\in\cC$. We measure the quality of such a family $\cC$ by either its size $\spr{\cC}$ or the min-sum time it takes to cover an item from $\cU$ (assuming some order of sets in $\cC$ closed under the precedence constraint). Depending on the cost objective we refer to the problem as the \textsc{Precedence Constrained Set Cover} ($\ProblemPCSC$) or the \textsc{Precedence Constrained Min-Sum Set Cover} ($\ProblemPCMSSC$).

We reduce precedence-constrained $\ProblemDT$ to these covering problems and develop approximation algorithms for them.

\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Optimal Decision Tree.}
\textsc{Optimal Decision Tree}, also referred to as \textsc{Binary Search Trees} in \cite{BoseCIKL20,DemaineHIP07}, is an extensively studied problem in computer science, starting with \cite{GareyPerfBoundsOnSplittingAlgForBinTesting} in the 1970s.
Since then it has gathered a lot of attention due to its numerous applications including medical diagnosis, troubleshooting, active learning, and information retrieval. 
Usually two optimization criteria are considered: the worst-case and the average-case cost.
Both versions of the problem are \NPhard and cannot be approximated within an $o\br{\log n}$ factor \cite{ApproximatingDecisionTreesMultiwayBranches,DiagnosisDetermination,ConstructOptimalBinaryDecisionTreesIsNPComplete,HardnessOfMinHeightDTP}, where $n$ is the number of hypotheses.
Moreover, this bound is tight and several $\bigo(\log n)$-approximation algorithms are known for the average-case both with non-uniform probabilities and test costs \cite{ApproximatingOptimalBinaryDecisionTrees,DTsforEntIdent,ApproximatingDecisionTreesMultiwayBranches,DiagnosisDetermination,MinimumCostAdaptiveSubmodularCover,AnalysisGreedyActiveLearning,AdaptivityInAdaptiveSubmodularity,AverageCaseActiveLearningWithCosts,ApproxAlgsForOptDTsAndAdapTSPProblems,OptimalSplitTreeProblem,AdaptiveSubmodularRankingAndRouting} as well as the worst case \cite{DecisionTreesForGeometricModels,TheCostComplexityOfInteractiveLearning,DiagnosisDetermination}. Few special cases are known to admit $o\br{\log n}$-approximation. For the average case, this includes an $\bigo\br{\log n/\log \log n}$-approximation when tests have a constant number of possible outcomes and all probabilities and costs are uniform \cite{TightAnalysisGreedyUniformDecisionTree}.
% This variant cannot be approximated within $\br{4-\epsilon}$ factor for any $\epsilon > 0$ unless P = NP \cite{DTsforEntIdent}. 
Moreover, obtaining approximation ratio of $\br{9+\epsilon}$ can be done in subexponential time, and thus is not \NPhard assuming ETH \cite{TightAnalysisGreedyUniformDecisionTree}.
For the worst case, when the underlying search space is a partially ordered set with one maximum element and costs are uniform an $\bigo\br{\log n/\log \log n}$-approximation is known \cite{EdgeRankingSearchingPartialOrders}.
To the best of our knowledge, the \textsc{Optimal Decision Tree} problem has not been studied in the presence of precedence constraints.

\paragraph{Set Cover with precedence constraints.}

\textsc{Set Cover} is among the most important problems in combinatorial approximation.
It is well known that the greedy algorithm achieves an $H_n$-approximation for \textsc{Set Cover} \cite{GreedyHeuristicSetCoverProblem}, where $H_n=\bigo\br{\log n}$ is the $n$-th harmonic number. This is tight since it cannot be approximated within a $(1-\epsilon)\ln n$ factor for any $\epsilon > 0$ unless P=NP \cite{AnalyticalApproachToParallelRepetition}.
The \textsc{Min-Sum Set Cover} is a variant of the problem in which the goal is to minimize the average coverage time of elements. For this version, the greedy algorithm is $4$-approximate \cite{ApproximatingMinSumSetCover}, which is tight.
When allowing arbitrary precedence constraints, $\ProblemPCMSSC$ admits an $\bigo\br{\sqrt{m}}$-approximation \cite{PCMSSC}. Earlier PDS-based work rules out polynomial-time $\bigo\br{n^{1/4-\epsilon}}$- and $\bigo\br{m^{1/8-\epsilon}}$-approximations \cite{OnApproxTargetSetSelection}. The construction and analysis below extend these bounds.

Section~\ref{sec:AL} gives an $\bigo(m^{1/3}\log^{4/3}m\log^2 n)$-approximation for both $\hyperref[problem:PCWCDT]{\ProblemPCWCDT}$ and $\hyperref[problem:PCACDT]{\ProblemPCACDT}$ under arbitrary precedence constraints, where $m$ is the number of tests and $n$ is the number of hypotheses. These general-precedence algorithms are randomized, with approximation guarantees holding with high probability. The results are obtained through reductions to partial set covering problems ($\hyperref[problem:PCSC]{\ProblemPCSC}$, $\hyperref[problem:PCMSSC]{\ProblemPCMSSC}$), which incur only a logarithmic factor loss. The idea is to find a precedence-constrained sequence of tests whose execution separates the hypotheses relatively well, and then recurse on the remaining subproblems. To that end we construct a corresponding covering instance, where the sets correspond to tests and the elements correspond to hypotheses.

The remaining task is to obtain the promised approximation for the precedence-constrained covering problems (cf. Section~\ref{sec:SC}).
We remark that there already exists an $\bigo(\sqrt{m})$-approximation for the \textsc{Precedence-Constrained Min-Sum Set Cover}~\cite{PCMSSC}, but it works only for $f=1$. As it turns out, we require $f=1/4$ for our decision tree algorithms to work.
To remedy this issue, we obtain improved, $\bigo(m^{1/3}\log^{4/3}m\log n)$-approximation algorithms for both cost criteria and all values of $f$. Firstly, we show that we can directly reduce $\ProblemPCSC$ to an instance of the previously known \textsc{Partial Red-Blue Set Cover} \cite{RedBlueSetCover} problem. However, the min-sum cost criterion turns out to be more challenging.
We introduce \textsc{Budgeted Precedence Constrained Set Cover} ($\ProblemBPCSC$), which asks for a precedence-closed family of at most $b$ sets that covers as many elements as possible. We reduce this problem to \textsc{Partial Red-Blue Set Cover}. For $\ProblemPCMSSC$, we construct a covering sequence by applying the approximation for $\ProblemBPCSC$ with exponentially growing budgets, so that sets covering many elements within a small budget appear early.

At last we concern ourselves with some special cases of precedence relations. For the inforests, where each test or set has at most one immediate successor, we show that $\ProblemBPCSC$ can be encoded as an instance of the well-known \textsc{Maximum Coverage}. This allows us to obtain an $\bigo(\log n)$-approximation for $\ProblemPCWCDT$ and $\ProblemPCACDT$. For the case of outforests, for which every test or set has at most one immediate predecessor, we show that $\ProblemBPCSC$ can be reduced to \textsc{Group Steiner Orienteering} on trees, which yields an $\br{\bigo\br{\log m}, 4}$-bicriteria approximation for $\ProblemBPCSC$, and as a consequence $\bigo\br{\log m\cdot\log n}$-approximations for $\ProblemPCWCDT$ and $\ProblemPCACDT$.
For this subcase, the connection between our problems and graph exploration becomes apparent, since any sequence of sets becomes a tour of the precedence graph.

\paragraph{Hardness Results.} 
Section~\ref{sec:hardness} provides two technical routes towards obtaining lower bounds. Firstly, we refine the construction of \cite{PCMSSC}, which maps graphs from the \textsc{Planted Dense Subgraph Conjecture} ($\ProblemPDS$) to precedence-constrained covering instances. We improve this construction by encoding $r$-cliques instead of graph edges and we give a sharpened probabilistic analysis, improving $\br{\bigo\br{n^{1/6-\epsilon}}}$ and $\br{\bigo\br{m^{1/12-\epsilon}}}$ lower bounds to bicriteria $\br{\bigo\br{n^{1/2-\epsilon}},\kappa}$- and $\br{\bigo\br{m^{1/4-\epsilon}},\kappa}$-inapproximability for both $\ProblemPCSC$ and $\ProblemPCMSSC$. These bounds hold for every fixed target fraction $f\in(0,1]$ and every fixed coverage-loss factor $\kappa\geq1$, even when the precedence DAG has height two.

We also give an entirely new reduction from \textsc{Label Cover}. 
In our construction the universe consists of the constraints of the \textsc{Label Cover} instance. For each vertex-label pair, a bundle of empty sets encodes choosing that label. A singleton witness set covers a constraint only after the bundles for a satisfying pair of endpoint labels have been selected.
Then, based on several hardness results and conjectures regarding \textsc{Label Cover}, we derive the following results for precedence-constrained coverings (Theorem~\ref{thm:label-cover-hardness}).
For every fixed target fraction $f\in(0,1]$ and $x\in\brc{n,m}$, neither covering objective can have a bicriteria $\br{\br{\log x}^{\bigo\br{1}},\br{\log x}^{\bigo\br{1}}}$ guarantee, unless $\text{P}=\text{NP}$. Parallel repetition rules out $\br{\bigo\br{2^{(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}},\bigo\br{2^{(\log x)^{1-\epsilon}}}}$-approximation for $0<\epsilon<1$ under ETH. Under the Projection Games Conjecture and $\text{P}\ne\text{NP}$, there is a constant $\delta>0$ for which neither covering objective admits a bicriteria $\br{\bigo\br{x^\delta},\bigo\br{x^\delta}}$-approximation. The covering instances have height-two precedence DAGs and sets of size at most one.

For the special case of outforests, for any $\epsilon>0$ we establish $\bigo(\log^{2-\epsilon} n)$-hardness of approximating $\ProblemPCSC$ via a reduction from \textsc{Group Steiner Tree} on trees, which is known to be hard to approximate within $\bigo(\log^{2-\epsilon} n)$-factor, unless $\text{NP}\subseteq \text{ZTIME}(n^{\text{polylog}(n)})$.
This naturally extends to $\ProblemPCWCDT$ via our reduction hierarchy. A similar result also holds for $\ProblemPCMSSC$, however here $f\neq 1$ and thus the reduction does not carry over to $\ProblemPCACDT$. See Table~\ref{tab:hardness} for a summary.
